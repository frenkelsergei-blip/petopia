PETOPIA Game Design Document
Game concept and implementation specification
Version 1.1 • 16 October 2026 • Development handoff edition
PETOPIA is a single-player virtual pet game for children ages 5–12. Players adopt a creature, care for it over real-world time, build a lasting bond, and explore original 2D pixel art adventures together as a child and pet team.
Build one complete loop first: adopt Foxy, care for it at home, play a minigame, explore Green Meadows, spend earned coins, and return to a home that remembers everything. Expand only after that loop is enjoyable and reliable.
The complete vision contains 12 pets, four living growth stages, 15 worlds and 75 main adventure levels. The first vertical slice contains one child, Foxy, one room, one minigame and one adventure. These are deliberately different production commitments.
For: A game developer, producer, artist, animator, audio designer or AI coding agent receiving the project without the original conversation.
Working tagline: Little Friends, Big Adventures
Design status: The core concept, named roster, world themes and four development phases preserve the referenced blueprint. Level concepts beyond The Lost Picnic, numerical tuning, technical contracts and acceptance tests are proposed specifications for implementation and playtesting. They do not represent tested game behavior or a committed production schedule.
Document guide
This specification connects creative direction to a buildable first milestone. Read the game pillars and growth rules before implementing systems; use the milestone gates and acceptance matrix to decide when a build is ready to expand.

1 Game identity and design pillars
PETOPIA centers the relationship between a child and a living companion. Home care changes how the pet responds, adventures give that relationship a purpose, and rewards return to the home as food, toys, clothing and decoration. The pet should remain interesting when the player stands still.

The relationship drives play
Every session should include a personal response: Foxy greets the child, Bubbles enjoys its favorite meal, or Ember proudly helps light a mechanism. Care is interactive and expressive, with recoverable needs and visible progress rather than punitive chores.
Adventures require cooperation
The child runs, jumps and interacts while the pet contributes an ability, discovery cue or puzzle action. Level goals include helping residents, retrieving lost objects and repairing environments. Explore and cooperate rather than relying on a repeated sequence of enemies and coin blocks.
Growth lasts beyond one session
Real-world elapsed time opens growth stages. Care, training and bond enrich development but cannot turn a newly adopted baby into an adult in a single afternoon. Missed days never kill a pet, erase a bond or remove essential progress.
Art communicates warmth and originality
Use rounded silhouettes, soft yet readable colors, expressive pixel animation, layered backgrounds and playful ambient details. Establish an original world, character language, hazards and level structure; avoid copying recognizable platformer characters, blocks or enemy patterns.
2 Core loop and first session
The two main modes are My Pet and Adventures. Minigames connect them with short activities that award food, coins, happiness, training or bond. The player can always choose something enjoyable while a pet rests or waits for its next growth stage.
Core loop: Adopt → care → play → adventure → earn rewards → customize and train → discover new possibilities → return home. Real-world growth progresses alongside this loop.
A proposed first session
1. Choose a child explorer and adopt Foxy. Enter a pet name or accept the default. Show a short greeting and save immediately.
2. Learn feeding by dragging a free starter meal to the bowl, or by selecting it using keyboard or controller. Foxy reacts and the fullness meter rises.
3. Clean one visible patch, play a short ball interaction and try the bed. Introduce needs through actions rather than a long tutorial.
4. Play Catch the Ball. Award a small participation result and explain that rewards can be used at home.
5. Select Green Meadows and enter The Lost Picnic. Learn movement, jumping, one interaction and Foxy’s treasure cue in context.
6. Return the picnic basket, receive a completion reward, visit the shop, buy a toy and place it in the room.
7. Return home, see Foxy use the toy, leave and reopen the game to recover the exact progress.
Starting target: Around 15–20 minutes for a relaxed first loop, with a 5–8 minute first adventure. These are design targets, not time limits. Allow pauses, repeat instructions and early return home.
Repeat sessions
On return, show a warm greeting, a concise needs summary and at most one new feature. Offer care, an adventure, a minigame and decoration without requiring all of them. Surface growth as a positive event when it becomes eligible, with an optional transformation celebration.

3 Child explorers and character direction
The player directly controls a child explorer. The full character concept includes four initial explorers with different hairstyles, clothing and visual identities. They share movement speed, jump behavior, collision size and ability access. Appearance should never imply a stronger or weaker choice.
The vertical slice uses one explorer. The other three are added after the controller, animation proportions and equipment layers have been proven. Give each explorer a readable side silhouette and a welcoming expression; leave personal names editable or use neutral presets selected during art approval.

Sprite and animation contract
Begin with a 32–48 pixel tall character at the chosen world resolution. Choose one common frame canvas large enough for jumping, hats and backpacks. Use stable feet and hand anchors across frames so clothing does not slide. Keep the collision body independent from decorative hair, hats and tails.
Required child actions are idle, walk, run, jump, fall, land, interact, celebrate, pet, carry, swim and a gentle hazard reaction. The slice requires all actions used in its actual flow; carry and swim can wait until a level uses them. A disabled feature should not appear as a working menu option.
Emotional acting
The child looks toward the pet when it discovers something, kneels during petting and celebrates with it after a goal. Petting and carrying should align to species and growth size. Use a shared event timeline so the child’s hand, the pet’s response and the sound land together.
Character sheet handoff
Produce front and side views for all four explorers, proportions, expression studies, a scale comparison with the three starter pets, equipment anchors, palette swatches and one representative movement cycle. Approve these as a coherent set before creating large clothing catalogues.

A complete species definition
Each species needs a unique silhouette, palette, facial expressions, movement identity, four living growth stages, one primary ability, a personality profile, favorite foods, favorite activities and cosmetic attachment rules. Record these as static definitions rather than duplicating them in every save.
Each owned pet has an individual name, adoption timestamp, growth progress, current needs, bond, training and equipped items. Switching the active pet should not reset other pets. Proposed full-game behavior keeps inactive pets safely resting at home with the same gentle offline care limits.
Adoption and collection
Offer the three starter species through a transparent choice in the core prototype, with no randomized purchase. The slice offers Foxy only. Later pets may be unlocked through world completion, collection achievements or a clearly displayed adoption goal. Show requirements before the player invests effort. No starter species should be permanently missable.
The collection screen displays owned pets, silhouettes for undiscovered species, growth stages seen and optional cosmetic discoveries. Avoid presenting adoption as a race to own the largest number of pets; make each relationship worth maintaining.

Four living stages
Baby, child, teen and adult are separate visual sets. Adoption or an egg is an introductory state, not a fifth living stage. Show continuity in eyes, palette and signature features. Adults should look like the same companion grown up, with changed proportions, markings, movement and expressions rather than a larger copy of the baby.
Behavior variation
Personality changes idle choices and reactions without making a pet unreliable. Foxy investigates a new object, Mochi observes before approaching, and Bubbles pauses with a relaxed blink. Low needs can alter pacing or expression, but a commanded ability still succeeds predictably when valid.
Production implication: The full roster requires 48 living-stage visual sets before costume combinations. The slice requires Foxy baby and child; the three-pet prototype requires 12 stage sets. Validate one species end to end before multiplying the animation workload.

The original day ranges are balancing targets. The exact thresholds above resolve inclusive day numbering: day 1 begins at adoption, so day 8 begins after seven full 24-hour periods. These values must be configurable for playtesting.
Proposed eligibility rule
Age determines the earliest eligible stage. A short stage interaction then lets the player celebrate growth. Care, bond, training and completed activities determine reactions, tricks and mastery within that stage; missed care does not freeze a pet’s biological age or permanently block its adulthood. If additional stage objectives are introduced, provide a short, freely available catch-up task.
On returning after a long absence, apply all elapsed age eligibility, then offer a concise growth recap. Persist the highest reached stage so clock changes cannot make the pet younger. Allow players to revisit earlier appearances cosmetically in a future album without changing gameplay age.
Clock handling
Use a monotonic clock for elapsed time within a running session and UTC for cross-session reconciliation. Apply each offline interval once. Clamp negative UTC deltas to zero and retain the prior highest observed timestamp so rolling back and restoring the clock cannot repeatedly award the same interval. Large forward jumps can advance eligibility in the offline MVP; elaborate anti-cheat is outside scope. Never punish the child for a clock correction.
Keep uncapped elapsed age separate from capped offline need decay. A pet left for 90 days becomes age-eligible for adult while its needs remain recoverable. On mobile resume, reconcile elapsed time before updating the visible pet.
Development verification
Provide an injectable test clock and a development-only age control. Test just before and at 7, 30 and 60 days, repeated loading, long absence, backward time and background/resume. The accelerated development clock must be absent from player-facing release settings.
7 Care and wellbeing
The pet has hunger, happiness, energy, cleanliness, health and bond. The first five are short-term needs; bond is the long-term relationship. Internally represent food satisfaction as fullness, with 100 meaning satisfied and 0 meaning hungry, so a “hunger 80” field cannot be misread as severe hunger. Label the child-facing meter “Food” with a bowl icon.

All numbers are proposed tuning. Clamp needs to 0–100. Apply offline decay for at most 12 hours per absence, with a proposed offline floor of 30; health never decreases offline. Bond never decays because the player is absent. Activity costs are shown in advance and cannot create a permanent care lock.
Feeding and cooking
Open the kitchen, choose a recipe, select ingredients, complete a short preparation interaction, serve and watch the response. Award care XP only after the meal is actually served. The slice can use a simple prepared-food interaction; Cooking Challenge expands this in the core prototype. Always offer a free staple meal and water when coins or ingredients reach zero.
Bathing
Show dirt patches, soap, bubbles, rinse and drying. Touch and mouse use broad swipes; keyboard and controller select a region and hold an action. Persist the cleaned result only once. Partial completion gives partial progress and can be resumed without buying supplies.
Sleep and health
Choose a bed and toy, dim the light and let the pet settle. Rest works while the player decorates or plays a pet-independent minigame. A free short nap can restore enough energy to continue an adventure. Low energy invites rest; it never blocks the whole application.
Occasional mild illness appears as a sleepy face or drooping posture, without frightening imagery. The pet always recovers through accessible care. No death, irreversible injury, paid medicine requirement or guilt-driven return message is permitted.
8 Bond training and cooperation
Bond is expressed through behavior first and numbers second. A newly adopted pet may look around cautiously. With repeated positive experiences it approaches the child, requests play, shares a high-five or performs a special greeting. The base ability is usable immediately; relationship progress adds expressive or optional enhancements.

These thresholds are proposed and should be configurable. Never remove a tier for missed days. Need state affects the current expression, not the stored relationship.
Award rules
As a starting balance, award 5 bond XP for a completed care interaction, 10 for meaningful shared play and 15 for a new cooperative discovery. Repeating the identical action rapidly still restores a need but grants reduced XP; use a modest per-action reward cooldown rather than a daily punishment. Keep completed-event IDs to prevent repeated rewards after reopening a result screen.
Care XP, bond XP and training XP are separate fields. A favorite meal can contribute to care and bond, but each award must identify its cause. A failed optional minigame still acknowledges participation; simply reopening and quitting cannot generate an infinite coin reward.
Training
Training is a short interactive routine: demonstrate, practice, respond and celebrate. Begin with a sit or spin trick, then practice the primary ability using safe targets. Do not require repetitive grinding to complete a main path.
Foxy’s training may increase cue radius or show a second sparkle; Ember may light two optional braziers; Bubbles may retrieve a second small item. Growth stages can add animation and mastery variants while leaving the child’s basic movement and essential level access intact.
Companion consistency
Personality and bond never cause a random refusal of a valid puzzle command. If an action is unavailable, show a friendly icon and a precise reason such as “move closer.” Always give visible feedback for command received, target selected, ability performed and objective resolved.
9 Side scrolling adventure systems
The child leads through a side-scrolling world and the pet follows through authored routes and recovery points. Use short, readable challenges with broad landing spaces, clear interactable silhouettes and optional exploration. Adventure hearts measure a level setback; they are separate from the pet’s health at home.

Map actions through InputMap so rebinding does not alter gameplay scripts. Keep interact and ability distinct, with a single target highlighted at a time. Touch must support movement and jumping simultaneously; focus loss clears held input.
Proposed movement targets
Start with ground speed near 120 world pixels per second, a jump around 48 pixels high and a 16 pixel tile grid; tune them together in a test room before laying out levels. Add roughly 100 milliseconds of coyote time and 120 milliseconds of jump buffering. These values are proposals, not evidence of a finished controller.
Companion state machine
Use follow, navigate obstacle, wait, investigate, ability, celebrate and recover states. Follow a short trail of the child’s safe ground positions; use authored jump links for gaps and platforms. A generic navigation agent alone is not a complete platform-jumping solution.
If the pet makes no useful progress for about two seconds, or falls far outside the camera, recover it to a validated nearby safe anchor with a small cloud effect. Do not teleport into hazards, walls or locked rooms. Reacquire the target after recovery. The pet should not push the child or block a doorway.
Gentle setbacks
Begin with three adventure hearts. A fall or hazard uses a brief non-graphic reaction and restores the team at a checkpoint. At zero hearts, offer an immediate retry from that checkpoint with a fresh heart pool. No loss of pet bond, purchased items or world completion. Prevent duplicate collectible rewards on retry by retaining per-run collected IDs.

Ability interaction contract
A target exposes supported tags, interaction position, current state, completion event and fallback route. The command checks range, line of interaction and target availability. Show a preview marker before committing. Use generous target selection and a proposed 3–6 second ability cooldown; never spend coins to activate a pet ability.
Temporary platforms warn before expiration. Hold them while the child is standing on them or provide a safe return surface. Save permanent puzzle results at checkpoints; reset temporary mechanisms predictably on retry. A cutscene cannot leave input, collisions or the companion state disabled afterward.
Example puzzle from the blueprint
At a broken bridge, Sprout grows a vine to an optional chest. A child-operated plank route reaches the same main exit. Foxy can instead reveal a nearby cache, Ember can light a lantern that marks a shortcut, and Bubbles can retrieve a submerged decoration. The selected pet meaningfully changes the discovery without determining whether the level can be finished.
Required level structure
Every level has a clear arrival, a readable goal, one distinctive mechanic, one cooperative pet moment, optional secrets, a reachable ending and a completion sequence. Add a checkpoint before a meaningful setback. Place the introduction to a new mechanic in a safe area before combining it with movement or timing.
Use introduction, exploration, puzzle challenge, special adventure and world finale as the five-level rhythm. A finale can be a rescue, restoration or celebration rather than combat. World unlocks depend on completion, not a pet reaching adult age or collecting every optional treasure.
11 Worlds one to three
The 15 world names and mechanic themes follow the blueprint. The named level concepts below extend that vision into a proposed 75-level catalogue. IDs are stable content references; layouts, timings and rewards remain subject to greybox testing. Each five-level set follows introduction, exploration, puzzle, special adventure and finale.
World 01 Green Meadows
Warm fields, soft hills and clear landmarks teach movement and collecting. Optional keepsakes are picnic stickers; petals, a distant windmill and shallow streams establish the palette.
W01 L01 The Lost Picnic: Follow a missing basket trail, cross a stream and use Foxy’s cue to find a hidden path. Recover the basket and share a picnic.
W01 L02 Windmill Walk: Explore a flower hill and ride slow platforms. Find a pet-marked cache while a broad lower path reaches the exit.
W01 L03 The Little Bridge: Collect three repair pieces and use a crank to span a brook. Pet shortcuts reveal one piece early.
W01 L04 Firefly Evening: Carry a lantern along a twilight trail and invite fireflies into safe light circles. Optional cues guide a secret meadow.
W01 L05 Meadow Gathering: Restore signs, guide picnic guests and reopen the clearing. Combine known mechanics in a generous, celebratory finale.
World 02 Sunny Beach
Bright shallows, shells and bobbing wooden pontoons introduce water and floating objects. The child can swim safely; Bubbles reveals optional underwater opportunities. Keepsakes are painted shells.
W02 L01 Shell Shore: Learn shallow swimming and stepping onto floats. Retrieve a beach flag while Bubbles finds a small submerged shell.
W02 L02 Driftwood Bay: Explore tide pools with slow rising water. Stable coastal paths let any starter reach the goal without diving.
W02 L03 Lighthouse Lens: Turn reflectors to guide a light through a beach cave. Pet retrieval provides a shorter route to a loose mirror.
W02 L04 Turtle Float Parade: Escort buoyant toy boats through gentle channels. Reset a stranded float with one accessible interaction.
W02 L05 Festival of Tides: Restore the harbor decorations and guide the final float home. Combine swim, float and light puzzles without a strict timer.
World 03 Mystic Forest
Layered leaves, glowing flowers and secret clearings center plant puzzles and hidden paths. Keepsakes are pressed magical leaves. Sprout enriches later visits without becoming mandatory.
W03 L01 Whispering Leaves: Follow rustling plants to a lost forest bell. Learn that flower silhouettes mark safe interactable objects.
W03 L02 Hollow Tree Trails: Explore branching tree routes and a ground-level passage. Narrow pet routes lead to optional keepsakes.
W03 L03 Seed Song: Arrange seed pods in a visible pattern to grow a staircase. Repeat the pattern freely and ask for a visual hint.
W03 L04 Moon Bloom Crossing: Open flowers in a calm sequence and cross wide leaf platforms. A vine ability creates an optional overlook.
W03 L05 Heart of the Grove: Reconnect three streams of light and awaken a friendly ancient tree. The reward restores a small forest garden.
12 Worlds four to six
World 04 Crystal Caves
Reflective stones, readable darkness and sparkling water create a gentle underground expedition. Use light and crystal mechanisms with shape cues as well as color. Keepsakes are crystal fragments.
W04 L01 A Spark in the Dark: Find a lantern and illuminate the path to a cave guide. Keep the walkable floor visible even without the lantern.
W04 L02 Echo Pools: Explore ledges around luminous pools and follow sound-and-shape clues. An optional submerged pocket rewards Bubbles.
W04 L03 Prism Path: Rotate three prisms to connect a beam to a door. Show both beam direction and receiver symbols.
W04 L04 Singing Stones: Repeat a short crystal sequence using visual pulses and optional audio. No sound-only requirement.
W04 L05 The Crystal Choir: Restore the cave’s central light sculpture. Combine beam alignment and platforming with a checkpoint before the final chamber.
World 05 Snowy Peaks
Soft snow, sheltered caves and sparkling ice teach slippery surfaces with predictable stopping areas. Frosty can freeze optional pools. Keepsakes are snowflake charms.
W05 L01 Snowboot Trail: Learn gentle ice movement on broad ground and return a lost scarf. Add safe snow patches before every edge.
W05 L02 Lantern Lodge: Explore a snowy hillside and relight trail markers. Ember offers an optional quick warm route.
W05 L03 Frozen Fountain: Move warm stones to free a waterwheel. Multiple visible controls ensure all starter pets can solve it.
W05 L04 The Sled Delivery: Guide a slow sled with parcels through broad lanes. Allow stopping and restarting with no race clock.
W05 L05 Aurora Lookout: Restore wind shelters and reach a viewing platform. The finale reveals an aurora and a shared photo pose.
World 06 Lava Volcano
Amber rocks, warm air and friendly workshop creatures use fire mechanisms and moving platforms. Lava is a clearly marked environmental setback with an immediate checkpoint return. Keepsakes are warm stone badges.
W06 L01 Warm Stone Steps: Cross slow platforms above glowing channels. Learn safe heat cycles with large resting surfaces.
W06 L02 Ember Lantern Trail: Explore lantern chambers; Fire Spark opens optional routes while hand-cranked lamps preserve main access.
W06 L03 Bellows Workshop: Redirect air and heat to restart a forge lift. Use symbols and an untimed planning phase.
W06 L04 The Floating Foundry: Ride stable stone rafts and recover a craftsperson’s toolbox. Every raft loop has a safe boarding point.
W06 L05 The Gentle Eruption: Reopen release vents to prepare a harmless light-and-spark display. Give unlimited retries at each station.
13 Worlds seven to nine
World 07 Sky Islands
Floating gardens, soft clouds and ribbons make wind direction readable. Introduce gliding and wind currents with safe recovery platforms. Keepsakes are feather tokens.
W07 L01 First Breeze: Ride a gentle updraft inside a wide practice valley. Sunny’s glide reaches an optional bell platform.
W07 L02 Kite Garden: Explore connected garden islands and gather kite ribbons. Permanent rope bridges serve the main route.
W07 L03 Wind Chimes: Turn fans to carry a seed to a receiver. A visible airflow ribbon explains each adjustment.
W07 L04 Cloud Courier: Deliver soft parcels between islands using wind lifts. Falling returns the parcel and team to the latest landing.
W07 L05 The Sky Regatta: Reconnect a floating parade route and guide its banner ship. Combine familiar winds with generous landing zones.
World 08 Desert Ruins
Warm sandstone, shaded courtyards and ancient mosaics support switches and hidden chambers. Avoid threatening heat meters. Keepsakes are mosaic tiles.
W08 L01 Footprints in Sand: Follow a friendly explorer’s trail to an oasis. Foxy marks buried optional mosaics.
W08 L02 Courtyard of Doors: Explore loops around a central courtyard. Doors reopen from both sides to prevent one-way traps.
W08 L03 Sun Dial Steps: Turn symbol wheels to cast shadows on a pictured sequence. All information remains visible during solving.
W08 L04 Sand Sail Journey: Ride a slow sail cart between ruin stops. Stop at each station to collect missing map pieces.
W08 L05 Oasis Restored: Reconnect water channels and return color to a garden. Every starter has a distinct optional discovery along the shared solution.
World 09 Dreamland

Shop categories
Food includes ingredients, meals and treats. Toys include balls, puzzles and interactive objects. Clothing and accessories cover hats, costumes, collars, glasses and backpacks. Furniture includes beds, chairs and shelves; decorations include plants, posters and carpets. Rare items should be cosmetic rewards with visible requirements.
Inventory and purchase rules
Use stable item IDs, a category, stack count where appropriate and an ownership record for permanent items. Preview a cosmetic on the active pet before purchase, show the coin price and use a single clear Buy action. Reject unaffordable purchases without changing either balance or inventory. Disable a purchase button while a transaction is in progress.
Debit coins and grant the item as one persisted state change. If saving fails, retain the last valid state and show a recoverable error. Prevent negative balances, repeated purchase events and duplicate one-time rewards. Equipping an owned item never charges again. Removing furniture returns it to inventory rather than destroying it.
Reward pacing and integrity
Daily activities and achievements can award coins, but no streak reset removes progress. Replays remain rewarding. Use action IDs to avoid accidental duplication, not punitive anti-cheat. The offline MVP accepts that a player can alter local data; correctness and recovery take priority over security theatre.
19 Home and customization
The initial home is one interactive room with a bed, food bowl, wash area and toy space. The pet wanders, approaches objects, settles to sleep, examines new furniture and greets the player. It should feel alive before decoration is extensive.

Durable save process
Serialize a validated snapshot to a temporary file under user://, verify it can be parsed, retain a known-good backup and replace the active save using supported file operations. PROJECT: PETOPIA
ROLE: Act as a senior Godot 4 developer, 2D gameplay engineer and game systems designer. Build a runnable, original pixel art virtual pet and side-scrolling adventure game for children ages 5–12. Use GDScript and pin a tested Godot 4.x version. Start with the vertical slice described below and carry it through a complete playable loop.
VISION: The player controls a child explorer, adopts a pet, cares for it over real-world time, develops a bond and explores colorful 2D levels with it. The pet must act as an expressive, useful companion. Home care, minigames, adventure rewards and customization form one connected loop. Use warm modern pixel art with consistent pixel density and an original visual identity.
FULL VISION: Twelve species are Foxy the fox, Mochi the cat, Bubbles the axolotl, Ember the dragon, Sprout the plant creature, Frosty the ice wolf, Bolt the robot, Luna the shadow creature, Poppy the bunny, Pebble the turtle, Sunny the bird and Cloudy the fantasy creature. The long-term game has four child explorers, four living stages per pet, 15 themed worlds, five main levels each and 75 main levels total. Do not build all of this in the first milestone.
FIRST MILESTONE: Implement one child, Foxy with baby and child visuals, one interactive home, feeding, cleaning, playing, sleeping, need and bond feedback, one minigame called Catch the Ball, one adventure called The Lost Picnic in Green Meadows, companion AI, Treasure Sense, collectible coins, a small shop, item ownership, one placeable toy, and reliable save/load. Provide keyboard and basic touch controls from the start. Use clearly identified placeholder art where final assets are unavailable.
CORE RULES: Pets never permanently die or disappear from missed care. Bond never falls because the player is absent. Free food, water, cleaning, play and rest remain available at zero coins. The player can do other activities while a pet rests. Main levels are completable with any starter pet; abilities add alternatives, secrets and shortcuts. Do not randomly refuse a valid ability command. No premium currency, paid speedups or mandatory login streaks.
GROWTH: Adoption is an introductory event; baby, child, teen and adult are the four living stages. Use proposed age thresholds of 0, 7, 30 and 60 full elapsed days since adoption. Age uses real-world elapsed time, including while closed, and cannot be skipped by grinding levels or coins. Care, training and bond enrich progress within stages. Stage appearances change more than scale. Provide an injectable test clock and development-only acceleration, never a release-facing age cheat.
CARE: Store fullness, happiness, energy, cleanliness and health as 0–100 values, plus separate care XP, bond XP and training. Higher fullness means well fed. Start with gentle configurable decay. Proposed offline behavior caps need decay at 12 hours and floors needs at 30; health never decreases offline and age remains uncapped. Use UTC for cross-session reconciliation, a monotonic clock within a session, zero negative deltas and a persisted timestamp high-water mark. Never apply an offline interval twice or regress a reached stage.
PLAYER: Use CharacterBody2D, readable ground movement, variable jumping if appropriate, jump buffering and coyote time. Proposed starting values are 120 world pixels per second, a 48-pixel jump, 100 milliseconds coyote time and 120 milliseconds buffering, tuned together in a test room. Inputs are move, jump, interact, pet_ability, companion_menu and pause through InputMap. Keyboard defaults are A/D or arrows, Space, E, Q, Tab and Escape. Touch must support move and jump simultaneously and clear held input after focus loss.
COMPANION: Use a state machine with follow, navigate obstacle, wait, investigate, ability, celebrate and recover. Follow safe ground positions and authored jump links. A generic navigation agent is not sufficient for platform jumping. Recover near the player after a configured stuck timeout using validated safe anchors, never inside a hazard or locked space. The pet cannot push the child or block progress. Foxy must visibly sense a nearby discoverable, perform its ability and reveal an actual reward or route.
THE LOST PICNIC: A friendly forest resident has lost a picnic basket. Start in a safe clearing. Teach walking, a small jump and coin collection. Let the child turn a crank to align stream-crossing planks. Place a checkpoint near the crossing. Foxy detects soft ground and reveals a shorter trail plus one optional secret; a visible longer path still reaches the basket. Collect the basket, open a return shortcut and bring it to the resident. Play a shared celebration, grant rewards and offer return home. Aim for a relaxed 5–8 minute level with no forced timer. Use three adventure hearts and gentle checkpoint recovery, with no home-health or bond penalty.
ECONOMY: Coins are the only spendable currency. Start with 50 coins per level completion, up to 20 optional pickups, a one-time 10-coin secret, a 15-coin paid meal, a 100-coin toy and a 300-coin costume. These are configurable proposals. Always provide free staples. Commit coin debit and item grant together; reject unaffordable purchases without mutation. Prevent duplicate reward claims with stable event IDs. Replays can award completion coins again, but unique discoveries grant once.
MINIGAME: Catch the Ball uses broad timing and clear visual cues, with keyboard, controller-ready actions and touch support. Include practice, pause, result, replay and home. Reward meaningful participation with happiness and bond; cancelling immediately does not generate rewards. A paused or backgrounded game cannot fail because its timer kept running. Create a reusable minigame result contract.
HOME: Include a bowl, bed, washing interaction and ball. Let Foxy wander, react, eat, play and settle to sleep. Add a small shop and a working toy placement flow with preview, confirm and cancel. Placement cannot block essential care or navigation. Removing furniture returns it to inventory. Equipment compatibility uses species, stage and attachment slots.
ARCHITECTURE: Use reusable scenes, modular components, signals and Godot Resources for static PetDefinition, ItemDefinition, AbilityDefinition, LevelDefinition and QuestDefinition data. Keep static definitions separate from individual pet state. Keep UI from directly mutating state. Use a small application service composition rather than unnecessary autoloads. Proposed services are GameManager, SaveManager, PetManager, PetNeedsSystem, PetGrowthSystem, BondSystem, InventoryManager, EconomyManager, QuestManager, LevelManager, CompanionAI, MinigameManager and AudioManager. Use TileMapLayer only if supported by the pinned version.
SAVE: Persist under user:// with schema_version, revision, time reconciliation data, active pet, owned pet records, coins, inventory, equipment, room layout, completed levels, discovery IDs, quest state, claim IDs, settings and checkpoint state where appropriate. Validate data on load. Write a temporary snapshot, verify it, preserve a known-good backup and replace the active save with ordered writes. Save after meaningful actions and before backgrounding. Recover corrupt primary saves from backup without silently resetting progress. Add migration support and test fixtures.
ART AND UI: Start from one coherent pixel scale; proposed world baseline is 640 by 360, 16-pixel tiles and 32–48 pixel tall children. Confirm on devices before bulk art. Use nearest filtering where appropriate and a separate responsive UI. Use large buttons, visible focus, short labels, non-color-only signals, visual sound equivalents, reduced motion and drag alternatives. Externalize text. Maintain an asset register with frame size, pivot, animation timing, anchors and licenses. Implement only animations needed by real mechanics, and identify remaining placeholders.
WORKFLOW: Inspect the existing project and applicable instructions. Briefly state the proposed architecture and exact scene hierarchy, then implement the authorized slice in small runnable increments. First prove movement and companion reliability, then home care, then the complete level, minigame, economy, customization and persistence. Integrate saving from the first persistent state. Test meaningful system boundaries and play the complete loop. Do not stop at a plan when implementation is possible. Do not claim completion for features that are absent or untested.
ACCEPTANCE: Demonstrate adoption and naming surviving restart; free care at zero coins; correct growth thresholds using a test clock; safe 90-day absence with bond preserved; no repeated offline award; keyboard and touch movement; safe companion recovery; Foxy revealing a real secret; checkpoint and level completion; minigame pause and results; one purchase and placement surviving restart; no duplicate or negative economy mutation; and recovery from a corrupt save. In Phase 2, validate every main path with Foxy, Ember and Bubbles. Record device and engine version, errors, observed results and limitations.
DELIVERABLES: Provide a runnable project, implemented features, created or changed files, scene hierarchy, input mappings, required assets, launch instructions, test instructions and results, known limitations and the next milestone. Phase 2 expands to three pets, four stages, three minigames and one five-level world. Phase 3 expands to six pets, three worlds, 15 levels and six minigames with complete mobile controls. Phase 4 grows toward all 12 pets and 75 levels after the earlier gates pass.
BEGIN NOW: Establish the Phase 1 project architecture, identify required placeholder assets, create the first runnable movement-and-companion scene, and continue toward the complete adoption-to-home loop.
